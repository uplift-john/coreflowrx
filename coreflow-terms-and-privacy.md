## 3. SMS Messaging Terms

### Program name

**CoreFlow Rx Patient & Provider Notifications**

### Program description

CoreFlow Rx operates two related messaging programs under this name:

**Patient notifications.** Patients who provide their mobile number receive appointment and
infusion-related messages — scheduling confirmations, infusion appointment reminders, nurse visit
windows, and changes to a scheduled visit.

**Provider office notifications.** Staff at referring provider offices who provide a mobile number
receive status updates on referrals they have submitted — referral received, benefits
verification in progress, prior authorization status, and ready-to-schedule notifications.

These are transactional and service-related messages tied to care already in progress. **We do not
send marketing or promotional text messages.**

### How you enroll

You are enrolled only when you affirmatively provide your mobile number and agree to receive text
messages — on a referral form, an intake or registration form, or by giving verbal consent to a
CoreFlow Rx staff member, which we document.

**Consent to receive text messages is not a condition of receiving services from CoreFlow Rx.** If
you prefer not to receive text messages, we will contact you by phone.

### Message frequency

Message frequency varies based on your care or referral activity. Patients typically receive
2–6 messages per month. Provider offices typically receive 1–5 messages per referral submitted.

### Message and data rates

**Message and data rates may apply.** Your mobile carrier's standard messaging and data charges
apply to messages you send and receive. CoreFlow Rx does not charge for this service.

### How to get help

**Text HELP** to the number you received the message from, and we will reply with support
information.

You can also reach us at **[MAIN_PHONE]** or **[SUPPORT_EMAIL]**.

### How to opt out

**Text STOP** to the number you received the message from at any time. You will receive one
confirmation message and no further texts from that program.

To rejoin, text **START** to the same number, or contact us at **[MAIN_PHONE]**.

**Opting out of text messages does not affect your care.** We will continue to reach you by phone
about your treatment.

### Supported carriers and delivery

This service is available on most major U.S. mobile carriers. Carriers are not liable for delayed
or undelivered messages. Message delivery is subject to your carrier's network and to
transmission conditions outside our control.

**Do not rely on text messaging for urgent or emergency matters.** For a medical emergency, call
911. For urgent clinical questions, call **[MAIN_PHONE]** or contact your prescribing provider.

### Privacy

Your mobile number and messaging consent are handled as described in our
[Privacy Policy](/privacy). **No mobile information is shared with third parties or affiliates for
marketing or promotional purposes.**

# IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

## 1. Split the two privacy documents — this is not optional

The current `privacy.njk` has five empty HIPAA NPP headings. **Do not put the SMS policy there and
call it done.** Structure:

| Route | Document | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| `/privacy` | Privacy Policy (Document 2) | Website visitors, carrier reviewers, SMS recipients |
| `/notice-of-privacy-practices` | HIPAA NPP (Stage 2 item R5) | Patients, surveyors, payers |
| `/terms` | Terms & Conditions (Document 1) | Everyone, carrier reviewers |

They must cross-link. A HIPAA NPP legitimately says health information *is* disclosed — to payers,
business associates, and for treatment. The SMS attestation says mobile data is *never* shared. On
one page those read as a contradiction, and a carrier reviewer scanning for the attestation will
either miss it or flag the conflict.

## 2. The consent language has to appear where you collect the number

Carrier reviewers check the form, not just the policy page. Wherever a mobile number is captured —
the Formstack referral workflow, intake forms, the contact form — the checkbox or field needs
language along these lines, and it must not be pre-checked:

> By providing a mobile number, you agree to receive text messages from CoreFlow Rx about
> appointments and referral status. Message and data rates may apply. Message frequency varies.
> Reply **STOP** to opt out or **HELP** for help. See our [Terms](/terms) and
> [Privacy Policy](/privacy).

This is a **Formstack build item**, not a website item — but the registration fails without it.
Add it to the same punch list as the BAA confirmation.

## 3. Both pages must be publicly reachable at registration time

Carrier reviewers fetch `/terms` and `/privacy` directly. **While Cloudflare Access is gating the
site, they will get a login screen and the registration will be rejected.** Before you submit:
either add a **Bypass** policy in Access for `/terms` and `/privacy` specifically, or complete
registration before enabling Access. Both pages must return `200` to an anonymous request.

Also link them in the site footer on every page — reviewers look for that.

## 4. Publish these before or alongside registration, not after

Order matters: pages live → consent language on the forms → submit the campaign.

## 5. Counsel review

This is guidance, not legal advice. Have counsel or your compliance consultant review both
documents before or shortly after publishing — particularly:

- Whether patient appointment reminders in your specific flows implicate TCPA prior-express-consent
  requirements beyond the HIPAA-related treatment exemption
- Retention periods against South Carolina Board of Pharmacy requirements
- Whether the service-provider carve-out in Section 4 accurately describes your actual vendor set
  (Formstack, WeInfuse, GoHighLevel, BrightStar Care)

One specific thing to confirm with counsel: GoHighLevel is your provider CRM and has no BAA.
Section 4's service-provider paragraph is written to be true only if no PHI reaches it. That
boundary is already a rule in `CLAUDE.md` — this policy now makes it a public representation.
